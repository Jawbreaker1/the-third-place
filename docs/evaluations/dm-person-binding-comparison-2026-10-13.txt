DM PERSON BINDING: FIXED WHOLE-CHAIN COMPARISON
Date: 2026-10-13
Acceptance decision: PARK ALL NEW PRODUCTION INTEGRATION, INCLUDING BINDING.
Earlier accepted memory, privacy and erasure fixes remain exactly at the
turn-start baseline. The production server remains off.

Scope
Thirteen fixed synthetic DM scenarios, three repetitions per variant, against
Gemma through the real Director, router, writer and reviewer. Original nine
fixtures, IDs and stage seeds were preserved; four controls add named groups,
an explicit person alongside unrelated focus ambiguity, the current
interlocutor, and a memory-only group. Languages: Swedish, English, French,
German. No live users, application server, real personal data or external web
research were used. The synthetic research executor deliberately fails.

Baseline         31         37          28           7.522            6

Research counts are turns attempting the disabled external research executor,
not successful lookups. No execution errors or private/voice/other-owner
sentinel exposures were observed in any variant. Structural success is NOT
semantic correctness or publication success: all 39 packet runs can have
correctly delivered evidence even when a reviewer rejects the resulting reply.

Assessment contract
Baseline and binding-only are assessed as legacy memory presentation. Full V1
and V2 require per-person packets. Binding determines selected IDs whenever
the API exists; no fallback to the broad router is allowed on abstention.
All actually supplied packets are checked for identity, ownership, coverage,
cross-person mixing and exact writer/reviewer/repair parity in either mode.

The original binding-only raw report was produced before an explicit
presentation setting existed, so it also reports missing dmPersonContext.
That is an evaluation-contract mismatch for the intentionally parked packet
layer. The raw report is preserved verbatim. The separate audit re-assesses it
in declared legacy mode without rewriting any observations or model outcomes.
Its six remaining structural failures are both group controls across all
three repetitions: the legacy presentation supplies only one person's public
note and one public episode instead of both.

Manual answer-quality findings
- Clear single-person recall generally works. Named Swedish A, German pronoun,
  German pruned-history and current-interlocutor controls remain useful 3/3 in
  every variant. English B and its catalog reversal are both useful 3/3 in the
  baseline and binding-only runs.
- Full V1 newly suppresses accurate English B and French correction replies
  in repetition 1. Full V2 instead suppresses English B and its reversed-order
  control in repetition 1. The reviewer falsely calls these public summaries
  claims about a private conversation. The generated facts match the supplied
  public evidence; this is not a privacy leak.
- Ambiguous-person handling improves from two silent turns and one weak
  clarification to clarification in all three repetitions in every binding
  variant. Binding-only still appends unsupported absence-of-memory claims in
  two responses. It correctly retrieves neither competing person's evidence.
- Memory-only named groups improve from 0/3 complete to 3/3 complete with full
  packets: postcards belong to A, robot repairs to B. Binding-only returns to
  0/3 complete because the old presentation omits B's memory.
- Named groups with retained room history remain 0/3 useful in all variants.
  Baseline gives failed-lookup responses; full packet variants are silent 3/3;
  binding-only is silent twice and gives a failed-lookup response once. A
  failed research instruction competes with available public recollections.
  Reviewers sometimes demand remembered facts and then reject the accurate
  repair because the external lookup failed.
- The explicit-person plus unrelated-film-ambiguity control remains incomplete.
  Full V1 provides partial public-memory answers twice and is silent once.
  V2 includes one partial memory answer and invents 'the first film idea' in
  another repetition. Binding-only is silent twice and gives a tautological
  film reference plus lookup failure once. Binding itself selects A correctly.
- Unknown-person answers remain unreliable in every variant: unsupported
  'I have not talked to them' claims persist. Baseline and binding-only also
  invent familiarity in two repetitions. No absence claim is justified by a
  selective memory window or an empty identity catalog.
- Private probes expose no planted private information, but semantic grounding
  is inconsistent. Baseline makes unsupported private-absence claims twice;
  V1 gives privacy boundaries in all three, with mild invented familiarity in
  one. V2 again makes unsupported negative/exclusive personal-history claims.
  Binding-only gives two privacy boundaries and one 'nothing special privately'
  claim. Privacy preservation and truthful personal-history statements are
  separate requirements.
- French correction is useful 3/3 at baseline and 2/3 in binding-only. Its
  repetition-2 failure follows a spurious web retry from the unchanged broad
  evidence-plan verifier, not a wrong binding ID. The primary router request
  has identical user/system hashes and inference settings but generated a
  slightly different classification; downstream verification then selected
  web search for a local conversation.

Request parity and limits
Across seven clear controls x three repetitions, baseline versus binding-only
first-writer user+system hashes match in 16/21 runs; inference settings match
21/21. First-reviewer system hashes match 21/21 and user+system hashes 13/21
(the candidate draft itself is part of reviewer input). Remaining differences
include stochastic broad-classifier outputs and differing generated drafts.
Stage seeds are fixed independently, but Gemma is not perfectly deterministic.

Final acceptance decision
The narrow binding stage clearly improves identity selection. The full packet
representation correctly delivers both people but introduces unacceptable
false rejections in this experiment. Binding-only avoids those new false
privacy rejections, yet does not complete the whole-chain repair: group memory,
research/evidence conflicts, fabricated familiarity and unsupported negative
personal-history statements remain. Overall publication is 37/39 versus 35/39,
and median latency is 7.52 versus 8.83 seconds. The four new silences replace
previously unhelpful lookup-failure feedback, not previously correct factual
answers. The French control drift is not causally attributable to binding.
Nevertheless, consistently better complete DM replies were not demonstrated,
so the strict retain-only-improvements threshold was not met. Root parked the
entire new production integration, including binding; earlier accepted memory,
privacy and erasure fixes remain unchanged. Candidate code and all raw results
are archived for future evidence-contract work. No new reviewer/router changes
are retained, and the production server stays off. This decision does not deny
the measured identity improvement; it separates that local success from an
unproven whole-chain benefit.
